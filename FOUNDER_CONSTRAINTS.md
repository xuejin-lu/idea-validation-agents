# Founder Constraints — Taiwan-First Startup Search

These constraints are authoritative defaults for startup research in this repository. They reflect the founder's explicit choices and must be applied before opportunity ranking or pilot recommendations.

## Market & Geography

- **Primary market: Taiwan only.**
- Do not recommend US-only, EU-only, or foreign-regulation-dependent opportunities as primary startup candidates.
- Foreign examples may be used as analogies or evidence only if the underlying problem clearly exists in Taiwan.
- Prefer Taiwan-specific evidence, pricing, workflows, regulations, buyer behavior, and distribution channels.

## Customer Access

- The first customers do **not** need to be personally known or physically reachable.
- Online access is acceptable.
- **Avoid business models that depend on cold calling, cold email, or aggressive outbound sales as the main acquisition method.**
- Prefer channels where potential users already gather or reveal intent:
  - search
  - marketplaces/directories
  - industry communities
  - LINE/FB groups
  - forums
  - app/service ecosystems
  - referrals
  - inbound content
  - partnerships
  - existing platforms

## Customer Type

- B2C, SMB B2B, enterprise B2B, and hybrid models are all allowed.
- Do not favor B2C or apps by default.

## Product / Business Type

- No required format.
- Software, SaaS, AI automation, services, marketplaces, hardware-enabled services, or other business models are all allowed.
- **A fully manual service is acceptable as the first version if someone will pay for it.**
- Productization and automation should come after evidence of demand.

## Budget & Validation Horizon

- Initial validation budget: **NT$0–5,000**.
- Acceptable validation period: **up to 3 months**.
- Prefer tests that cost less and finish earlier when they produce equally strong evidence.
- Do not recommend custom hardware, paid ads, large datasets, or expensive integrations before cheaper tests are exhausted.

## Revenue Threshold

- No minimum revenue target is required for the first validation.
- **Any real payment is meaningful evidence** if it comes from a real target customer.
- Prefer evidence of payment or commitment over surveys or stated interest.

## Market Size

- A market does **not** need to be large.
- A small or niche market is acceptable if:
  - pain is strong,
  - customers are reachable,
  - competition is manageable,
  - margins can be healthy,
  - a tiny team can serve it.

Do not reject a niche solely because TAM is small.

## Domain Difficulty

The founder is willing to:
- spend **1–3 months learning a domain**,
- enter a high-barrier industry if the economics justify it,
- use existing EE/software/technical capability when it creates an advantage.

Therefore:
- do not exclude complex domains automatically,
- but explicitly price the learning, trust, licensing, and access cost.

## Regulation

- Regulated markets are allowed.
- Regulation may be a moat.
- However, never recommend a regulated opportunity without identifying:
  - Taiwan-specific legal/compliance requirements,
  - whether licenses/certifications are required,
  - whether the founder can legally test the service before full compliance,
  - trust and procurement barriers.

## Technology Philosophy

- Technology is a means, not the objective.
- AI is **not required**.
- Do not prefer an AI idea over a non-AI idea unless AI materially improves unit economics or customer value.
- Do not invent a technical product merely because the founder has technical skills.

## Exclusions

- No category is automatically excluded.
- Hardware, apps, social products, e-commerce, consulting/services, medical, and finance are all allowed.
- Evaluate each on evidence and practical accessibility.

## Search Philosophy

Use both:

1. **Accessible Pain First**
   - Start from real problems in Taiwan that can be observed, researched, or reached online.

2. **Founder Advantage**
   - Give extra weight to opportunities where technical skill, EE/software knowledge, research ability, or fast prototyping provides an advantage.

Neither rule overrides commercial evidence.

## Hard Filters

A candidate should usually be rejected or deprioritized if:

- the problem is mainly foreign-market-specific,
- the first 10 target customers cannot realistically be found online in Taiwan,
- customer acquisition depends primarily on cold outreach,
- validation requires > NT$5,000 before any meaningful signal,
- validation requires more than 3 months before a behavioral signal,
- the opportunity needs custom hardware or full software implementation before demand can be tested,
- the buyer/problem cannot be verified with Taiwan-specific evidence.

## Preferred Candidate Shape

The ideal first-test opportunity looks like:

> A specific Taiwan-based user or buyer with a recurring painful workflow, visible online, using an inefficient workaround today, where a manual or lightweight technical intervention can create measurable value and obtain the first payment within 3 months for ≤ NT$5,000.

## Decision Rule

Do not ask:

> "Is this a large market?"

Ask:

> "Can we find a real Taiwan customer with this pain, reach them without heavy outbound sales, provide value manually or cheaply, and obtain a real payment or commitment within three months?"
